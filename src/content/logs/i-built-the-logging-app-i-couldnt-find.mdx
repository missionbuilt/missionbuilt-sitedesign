Walk up to the bar. Do the set. Get it down before the next one.

IronStack is the logging app I built so that's all it takes. It's for lifters who are tired of fighting their log, including the ones who log in Hevy or Strong today.

## What you get

- **Last time's numbers are already in the set.** Most sets are one tap.
- **The Apple Watch app runs the workout.** Log the set, run the rest clock, leave the phone in the locker. Turn the Crown to change a number. With chalked hands it's the best way, and it's how I log now.
- **Add is always the first row.** Type a lift the library doesn't have and the first thing you see is how to add it.
- **Distances in their own units.** Feet for a sled push, metres for a row.
- **A program follows where you are, not the calendar.** Miss a Tuesday and nothing breaks.
- **Free to log, with no caps on programs, workouts or exercises.** No ads. No feed. Pro adds Stack AI and your whole history, and [the pricing page](/pricing) has the rest.
- **Ask your log.** Get answers from your whole history, not one app's slice of it.
- **Social is the people you invite.** I share my lifts with friends and back theirs.
- **Coach mode.** I help my son with his lifting, and that's why I built it.

<Aside tag="Coming from Hevy or Strong?">

Nothing signs in anywhere, so you can run both for a couple of weeks and go back if you like. IronStack is for iPhone and Apple Watch.
</Aside>

## Why I built it

I have used a lot of logging apps. Every one was missing something, and the something was always small:

- The add button I couldn't find.
- A free plan that held a few routines, when my program was eight weeks long.
- Ads and a feed between sets, when I only opened the app to log one.
- A loaded sled push the app wanted in reps, when it's feet and weight.

Then there were two apps. I train powerlifting in JuggernautAI. I like it and I recommend it. When I wanted to weave in strongman, there was no good way to do it there, so I picked up the Shaw Elite app. Two logs of one body of training.

What I needed was one place to log, with my data in my hands, whatever program I was running. I still subscribe to both apps and use both. IronStack is where the log lives.

## The phone is the problem

The moment it's in the gym, distractions creep in. Texts. Slack. Email. I work a distributed job with people across the world, so the messages never stop. Focus modes help, and I use them. The real fix is going fully to the watch:

> Just the set, the rest clock and the next set.

A watch app that's only a remote for the phone leaves the phone in your hand. This one doesn't.

## If it's training, it belongs in the log

Most strongman carries aren't covered in the traditional apps: yoke, frame, farmers. You track distance and weight. You can create each one by hand. I wanted one place where all of it is managed together.

That goes past strongman. People in my house do Pilates, and we do yoga for flexibility and conditioning. My son adds basketball and lacrosse to his lifting. It's all training, so it all goes in one log, cardio and rucking included.

## Who's behind it

I'm Mike. I served in the U.S. Army as an airborne intelligence analyst, in support of Operation Enduring Freedom and Operation Iraqi Freedom in the early 2000s. Mission Built is veteran-owned.

The Army taught me dedication: keep to the workouts, and on a bad day, just move a bit. That's why IronStack has so many ways to train. A bad day should still have a place in the log.

I started using AI more at work, and somewhere in there it clicked that I could build the app I'd been wishing for. I wrote [*Mission Built: a field guide for building things that matter*](/chapters) first, then shared the skills I use. The app came after. How I built it is the next post.
