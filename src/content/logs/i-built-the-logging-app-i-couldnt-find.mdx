Here is what I want from a logging app. Walk up to the bar. Do the set. Get it down before the next one. Nothing else.

That's the whole job. It's also the job most of them get wrong.

## The moments

I have used a lot of logging apps. Every one was missing something, and the something was always small.

I train powerlifting in JuggernautAI. I like it and I recommend it. It rebuilds your workouts around how you perform, and it does that well. Then I wanted to weave in strongman, and there was no good way to do it there. So I picked up the Shaw Elite app for the strongman work.

Now I had two apps and two logs of one body of training. What I needed was one place to log, with my data in my hands, whatever program I was running.

IronStack is that place. It isn't a replacement. I still subscribe to both apps and use both.

The add button I couldn't find. The free plan that held a few routines, when my program was eight weeks long. The ads and the feed between sets, when I only opened the app to log one. A loaded sled push that the app wanted in reps, when it is feet and weight.

And the strongman work. Most carries aren't covered in the traditional apps: yoke, frame, farmers. You track distance and weight. Yes, you can create each one by hand and add it yourself. I wanted one place where all of it is managed together.

That goes past strongman. People in my house do Pilates. We do yoga for flexibility and conditioning. My son adds basketball and lacrosse to his lifting. It all counts as training, so it all belongs in the same log.

Then the phone. The moment it's in the gym, distractions creep in. Texts. Slack. Email.

I work a distributed job, with people across the world, so the messages never stop. If I don't force a disconnect, I get pulled out of my workout. Every time.

Focus modes help, and I use them. But the real fix is going fully to the watch. No pull toward social media or Reddit. Just the set, the rest clock and the next set.

That keeps the mind-muscle connection. It keeps me focused and motivated. A watch app that was only a remote for the phone kept the phone in my hand, so it never fixed this.

Small things. Every set, every week.

Small things compound. In the gym and in software.

## Why now

So why build it myself, and why now?

I started using AI more at work. Somewhere in there came the aha moment: I might finally be able to build something that fits exactly what I need.

It was the right place and the right time. First came my book, [*Mission Built: a field guide for building things that matter*](/chapters). Then the skills I started to share. An app was the natural next rep.

Not a hype moment. A progression.

## What it is meant to solve

Less friction between sets. That's the whole product.

So when you open a workout you have done before, each working set already holds what you lifted last time. Most sets are one tap. Add is always the first row: type a lift the library doesn't have and the first thing you see is how to add it. Distances read in their own units, feet for a sled push, metres for a row. A program follows where you are, not the calendar. Miss a Tuesday and nothing breaks.

The Apple Watch app runs the workout. Log the set, run the rest clock, leave the phone in the locker.

Logging in IronStack is free, with no caps on programs, workouts or exercises. No ads. No feed.

One log also means better questions. Ask about your lifts and get answers from your whole history, not one app's slice of it.

Social is the people you invite and the people who invite you. I share my lifts with friends and back theirs. I also track my son's lifting so I can help coach him.

## How it was designed

Every screen traces to a moment in the gym. If I couldn't name the moment, the screen didn't get built.

A few rules I hold it to, in plain words:

- A set is one entry. The app never logs a set for you.
- Never hide the add button.
- A workout is called a workout, everywhere.
- No paywall in the middle of a workout.
- Your log stays yours, on your phone. No usage analytics.

Here is the honest part. Two of the worst moments this year came from my own beta. I typed a lift the library didn't have and couldn't find the add button. I finished workout 3 and the app offered me workout 1 again. Both were fixed in the next build, and the add button became a rule for every app I make.

Two more changes came from using it. First, remembering the last lift. I realized quickly that the app had to open each set on what I lifted last time, so I stopped retyping it. Second, the watch. At first it was taps and voice. Then, with chalked hands, I found the crown: turn it to change the number. It became the best way, and it's how I log now.

Exercise Info now carries a card for 410 lifts: how to set up, how it should feel, the usual miss, and the cues. I checked each one against coaching sources before I approved it.
